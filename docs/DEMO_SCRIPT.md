# Mlinzi — demo video script (v2, ~105 seconds)

Two commands, one continuous narration. Record the terminal; let text
scroll behind your voice rather than pausing for each block.

```
./mlinzi show ke-police-misconduct
./mlinzi demo-full
```

---

**[0:00 – 0:15] — Before running anything. Black terminal, or terminal with prompt only.**

> "Someone's stopped at a stage in Kisumu. An officer wants two hundred
> shillings to let them pass. This happens constantly — and here's the real
> problem: they don't know IPOA exists, don't know it's free, don't know it
> works without airtime. Nothing gets reported. Not because people don't
> care — because the information gap comes first."

**[0:15 – 0:20] — Type and run `./mlinzi show ke-police-misconduct`. Let it scroll to the institutions section.**

> "This is Mlinzi answering that gap. Sourced. Dated."

**[0:20 – 0:35] — Pause on or circle back to the IPOA channel lines (the two disputed toll-free numbers).**

> "And here's something we didn't design — we found it. Two official
> sources publish two different IPOA hotlines. So we show both, flagged,
> instead of picking one and hoping. That's not a feature we invented. It's
> what happened when we actually checked."

**[0:35 – 0:45] — Cut to `./mlinzi demo-full`, let Step 1 and Step 2 print.**

> "Knowing isn't the hard part. Reporting is — because it's traceable, and
> sometimes it's dangerous. This report is hash-chained the second it's
> filed. No name required. And no institution, including the one receiving
> it, can quietly edit or delete it afterward."

**[0:45 – 1:05] — Let Step 3 print through case creation and escalation.**

> "If reporting puts someone at risk, they set a dead man's switch. Their
> evidence is split, cryptographically, across guardians. No single
> guardian — and not us either — can unlock it alone."

**[1:05 – 1:20] — Let the release lines print.**

> "Go silent, and two guardians acting together — only together — can
> release it. Silence stops being a way to make a report disappear."

**[1:20 – 1:30] — Let the final chain-verification lines print.**

> "Every one of these steps is independently checkable. Not a claim in a
> deck — a command anyone can run."

**[1:30 – 1:45] — Closing. Terminal still visible, or cut to the repo page.**

> "Back to that officer at the stage. Now there's a path — and if the path
> is dangerous, there's a floor under it. Built with AI tools, logged
> honestly, including the bugs we caught. Novel because it's cryptographic,
> not just another form. Scalable because a new country is a data folder,
> not a rewrite. Mlinzi. Know. Report. Be protected."

## Notes for recording

- Increase terminal font size before recording; judges may watch on a
  laptop at reduced video quality.
- A dark terminal theme reads better on video than default white.
- The IPOA disputed-number lines are easy to miss if you don't slow down —
  consider pausing playback or zooming in briefly when they're on screen,
  since that single moment is the strongest evidence of genuine research in
  the whole video.
- Record one full unedited take first as a safety net, then a polished take.
- Both commands complete in under a second of real runtime — you are
  narrating over a paused or slowed scroll-through, not real-time output.
  Add short pauses (or replay slowly) between sections so the terminal
  paces with your voice rather than racing ahead of it.
- Cut the closing criteria line short if you're tight on time — the loop
  back to the officer ("now there's a path...") is the more important half
  of the close.
